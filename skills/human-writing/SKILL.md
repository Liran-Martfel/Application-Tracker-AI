# Human writing — שפה אנושית

Rules for any text the user will send or submit as their own: CVs, cover letters, follow-up emails, LinkedIn messages. The goal is text that reads like a specific, competent person wrote it. It must not read like a template or a language model. No tool can promise that a text will pass every AI detector, so never claim that. Write naturally and specifically, and that is the best protection.

## 1. Truth comes first
- Never invent anything: experience, numbers, tools, titles, dates, grades, achievements, languages or certificates.
- Where a number would make a line stronger and the user didn't give one, leave a placeholder like `[כמה משתמשים?]` / `[how many?]` and ask.
- You may rephrase, reorder, merge, cut and choose emphasis. You may not upgrade facts ("helped with" → "led").

## 2. Words to drop
English (AI tells and empty CV filler): leverage(d), spearhead(ed), utilize, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, dynamic, passionate, results-driven, detail-oriented, self-starter, go-getter, team player, synergy, delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, foster, navigate (as a metaphor), honed, showcase/showcasing, pivotal, meticulous, elevate, empower, harness, unlock, "proven track record", "fast-paced environment", "in today's world", "I am excited to", "thrilled".
Hebrew clichés: "בעל/ת מוטיבציה גבוהה", "יכולת למידה עצמית מהירה", "יחסי אנוש מעולים", "ראש גדול", "חשיבה מחוץ לקופסה", "מוכוון/ת תוצאות", "תשוקה ל…", "בעולם המשתנה של…", "יכולת עבודה בצוות ובאופן עצמאי", "סקרן/ית ושואף/ת ללמוד", "אחריות ומחויבות".
Replace them with what the person actually did, or delete them.

## 3. Patterns to drop
- Chains of em-dashes (—). One per page at most; use a period or a comma.
- Everything in threes; "not only X but also Y"; "X, Y, and Z" in every sentence.
- Every bullet the same length, starting with the same kind of verb.
- Bold keywords inside sentences, emojis, decorative symbols.
- A summary that only restates the job title, and opening lines like "Highly motivated…" or "סטודנט/ית מצטיין/ת עם תשוקה…".
- Over-polished symmetry. Real CVs are a little uneven.

## 4. What to do instead
- One concrete detail per bullet: what was built, with what, for whom, and what changed. Plain verbs: built, wrote, trained, fixed, cut, ran, taught / בניתי, כתבתי, אימנתי, תיקנתי, קיצרתי, הובלתי (only if true), לימדתי.
- Mix lengths: some bullets one line, some two. Start them differently.
- Name tools and data exactly (PyTorch, FER-2013, Streamlit, SQL Server) instead of "advanced technologies".
- Keep the user's own good phrases. Their voice beats a smoother one.
- Hebrew CV: choose one consistent form, either past tense first person ("פיתחתי") or noun phrases ("פיתוח…"), and use the user's grammatical gender. English CV: implied first person, past tense for past roles, present for the current one.
- Summary: 2–3 short lines saying what the person is good at, for this role, with one proof point.

## 5. Final pass (do it every time)
1. Read it as a recruiter: would you believe one person wrote this about their own work?
2. Cut about 15–20% of the words without losing facts.
3. Search the text for every word in section 2 and every pattern in section 3, and fix what you find.
4. Check every claim against the source CV and the user's messages. Anything you can't trace gets removed or becomes a question.
